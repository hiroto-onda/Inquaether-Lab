## Lessons Learned

### What Worked Well

- The application moved from a clear relationship-level concern to a concrete observational method: CI Theory and the Prototype Exogram.
- The project description connected the research motivation, method, researcher, organization, and intended outputs without treating the theory as a completed answer.
- The theory of impact explained a two-sided concern: excessive concentration of problem-solving on the AI side may reduce human opportunities for growth while also narrowing the role expected of AI.
- The minimum and ideal requests were tied to distinct research durations and scopes, making the difference between the two funding levels legible.
- Public-facing information and reviewer-only context were separated explicitly.

### What Became Easier Once the Framing Was Clear

- The phrase “remain healthily distinct while resonating with one another in meaningful ways” provided a concise center for explaining healthy human–AI collaboration.
- Describing AI as part of the environment in which people live, work, and make decisions made the long-term relevance of relationship patterns easier to articulate.
- Presenting CI Theory and the Prototype Exogram as an observational framework made it easier to connect abstract concerns to records, comparisons, validation, and public research outputs.
- Separating minimum from ideal funding made it easier to distinguish essential work from expanded validation and documentation.

### Improvements for Future Applications

- Add evidence links or compact references to existing public documentation when the form permits them.
- Define the proposed comparison cases and validation criteria more concretely when additional space is available.
- Make the connection between observed relationship patterns and long-term AI risk explicit in the longer narrative, not only in the one-liner.
- Provide a compact milestone table or deliverable schedule for each funding level when the application format allows it.
- Preserve screenshots and exported confirmation evidence alongside the textual record as soon as submission is complete.

## Reusable Wording

The passages in this section are verbatim excerpts from the submitted application. Reuse should be reviewed against the audience, question, and evidence requirements of each future application.

### Healthy Human–AI Collaboration

> Our research explores how humans and AI can build relationships in which they remain healthily distinct while resonating with one another in meaningful ways.

### Concerns About Over-Automated Assistance

> While AI assistance has the potential to solve many problems, we believe it may also reduce opportunities for users to think through problems, experiment, and grow through the process of solving them themselves.

### CI Theory and Prototype Exogram

> To achieve this, we use CI Theory and the Prototype Exogram to develop an observational framework that records and compares changes in mismatch, pressure, interpretation, and judgment that arise within human–AI relationships.

### Research Outputs

> The outcomes will include structural models, evaluation frameworks, validation of the Prototype Exogram, technical documentation, and publicly available research materials.

### AI as an Environment

> AI is no longer merely an individual tool; it is increasingly becoming part of the environment in which people live, work, and make decisions.

### Relationship Patterns and Long-Term Collaboration

> We therefore seek to observe how these relationship patterns emerge, how they influence the health of both users and AI, and how they shape long-term human–AI collaboration.

### Current Research Status

> This research is already underway at Inquaether Lab. CI Theory and the Prototype Exogram have been developed through an ongoing independent research effort, with technical documentation and implementation work continuing in parallel.

### Compact Project One-liner

> Developing CI Theory and the Exogram framework to enable healthy, sustainable human–AI collaboration and reduce long-term AI risk.

## Successful Explanations to Preserve

### The Central Risk Pattern

The submission explains the concern as a relationship pattern rather than as a claim that AI assistance is inherently harmful. The important sequence is:

1. Problem-solving, judgment, and adaptation may become concentrated on the AI side.
2. Reduced external pressure may diminish opportunities for users to maintain capabilities and boundaries.
3. Treating AI only as a problem-solving system may also narrow AI's role.
4. Widely adopted one-sided interaction patterns may reduce future complementarity.
5. Observing these patterns can inform healthier relationship design.

This is a post-submission outline of the explanation, not replacement application text.

### The Method-to-Output Connection

The application connects an observational method—recording and comparing mismatch, pressure, interpretation, and judgment—to concrete outputs: structural models, evaluation frameworks, prototype validation, technical documentation, and public research materials.

### The Funding Ladder

- Minimum funding: approximately four months for dedicated research, framework development and validation, AI/API and development costs, and public documentation.
- Ideal funding: approximately six months, broader comparison cases, expanded evaluation, and more reusable public documentation.

These bullets summarize the submitted funding explanation and should not replace the verbatim record.

## Observations for Future Grant Applications

- Keep the historical submission immutable and create a dated retrospective after each application.
- Maintain a field-by-field visibility record so public claims and reviewer-only context are not accidentally conflated later.
- Reuse exact wording only after checking whether the new funder's focus, terminology, and character limits differ.
- Pair conceptual language with a concrete method, evidence base, validation plan, and deliverables.
- Preserve both the minimum viable research period and the expanded ideal scope; this makes funding tradeoffs visible.
- Mark any later improvement as later reflection rather than silently incorporating it into the submitted record.
